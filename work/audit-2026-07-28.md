## §0. Method — the two accounting facts that had to be established first

Both were wrong in my first pass and are now pinned by evidence. Everything downstream depends on them.

**Fact 1 — Kalshi fills are always *acquisitions*; there is no "sell".**
The fills tape contains only `(buy, yes)` and `(sell, no)` — no `(buy, no)`, no `(sell, yes)`. Decoded:

| tape row | economic meaning | cash | Δ position (YES axis) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `action=buy, side=yes` | acquire q YES at `yes_price` | −q·yes_price | +q |
| `action=sell, side=no` | acquire q NO at `no_price` | −q·no_price | −q |

Closing a position is not a sell — it is buying the opposite side, which the exchange **nets**, releasing
$1.00 per offsetting pair. Netting released pairs = `min(Σyes, Σno)`.

**Validation (not assumption):** across all 30 markets the model reproduces the exchange's own
`position_fp` **30/30** and `total_traded_dollars` **30/30**; the netting identity
`−spend + pairs = −market_exposure + realized_pnl` holds **30/30**. The naive "sell credits cash" reading
fails on 19 of 30 markets — it was inverting the sign on every NO acquisition.

**Fact 2 — settlement `fee_cost` is a restatement, not a new charge.**
On all 9 settlements carrying a fee, settlement `fee_cost` equals the sum of that market's fill fees to the
cent (diff `0.0000` on every one; total $1.8434 both ways). Subtracting it at settlement double-counts.
Also: the exchange's `realized_pnl_dollars` on the positions endpoint is **gross of fees** — fees must be
subtracted separately (verified on KXDXYDUD-26JUL27: −64.4055 spend + 93.00 pairs = +28.5945 exactly).

**Fill-set completeness:** for all 23 settled markets, Σ fill counts equals `yes_count_fp + no_count_fp`
from the settlement record exactly. The tape is complete over the window; no pagination gap.

### What the table supports, and what it does not

**Direction: confirmed, weakly.** On the 8 ladder events (≥5 rungs available) with material P&L or accrual,
Spearman **ρ = +0.333**, Kendall **τ = +0.214** (17 of 28 pairs concordant). Higher coverage does associate
with better outcomes. The two best-covered real ladders (gas 26JUL28 at 0.59, gas 26JUL29 at 0.47) are the
two profitable ones; the two worst-covered (PYPL 0.25, UST10 0.27) include the worst loss.

**Thresholds: not reproduced.** The "winners cover ≥2/3, losers under half" formulation does **not** hold on
this window's data, for a concrete reason: **no ladder event reached 2/3 coverage.** The best was gas
26JUL28 at 0.59 (10/17). And the rule misclassifies in both directions — UST5AD covered 0.53 (above half)
and lost $6.13; UST10AD covered 0.27 (under half) and made $1.18.

**The mechanism the heuristic is pointing at is real, and it is worth stating precisely**, because it is
more useful than the ratio: a strike ladder is monotone — only one rung can be "the money". Covering a
contiguous majority of rungs makes the positions hedge each other, and the settlement outcome lands inside
your book instead of outside it. Gas 26JUL28 with 10/17 had the settlement print (between 4.095 and 4.100)
*inside* its covered range; PYPL with 4/16 did not. **Coverage matters because of where the settlement lands
relative to the covered band, not because of the ratio itself.** A 2/3 ratio spread across the wrong end of
the ladder would not have helped.

Recommended canonical form going forward: record coverage **and** whether the settlement print fell inside
the covered rung range. That is the testable version of Ryan's intuition, and this window gives it 2 for 2.

**Caveat on the denominator:** `avail` is measured from the live program table at T. For events whose
programs have already expired, availability may be undercounted; the 26JUL28/26JUL29 gas (17) and UST (15)
figures agree with R176/R177's independently-measured ladder sizes, so the material rows are sound.
